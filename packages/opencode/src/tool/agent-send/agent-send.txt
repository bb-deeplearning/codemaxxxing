Queue a plain-text message into another agent's mailbox. Fire-and-forget: the
message lands in the recipient's inbox immediately, but the recipient does NOT
wake up. They will see your message at the start of their next natural turn,
when they drain their entire mailbox in delivery order.

Use send_message for context updates, FYI notes, and status that the recipient
should know about but does not need to act on right now. Examples: "I finished
the parsing pass — schemas land in src/parser/schema.ts", "skip the redis
branch, we're going with sqlite", "blocking on @worker_b before I can verify".

Do NOT use send_message when you actually need the recipient to do something
next. For that, use followup_task — it queues the same way AND triggers a turn,
so the recipient picks up the work without waiting on whatever else they were
doing. send_message is the wrong tool whenever your message ends with a
question or a request.

Mailbox semantics:
- Multiple senders interleave in delivery order. The recipient sees your
  message alongside any others queued since their last turn.
- Messages persist until drained — sending to an idle agent is fine; they
  read it whenever they next run.
- The recipient sees who sent each message; you do not need to identify
  yourself in the body.

Targets resolve the same as every other multi-agent tool: a bare task name
("worker_b") resolves relative to your current path; a canonical path
("/root/explorers/worker_b") is absolute. Either works.

## correlation_id (optional)

Set when you want the recipient's `wait_for_reply` call to wake ONLY on your matching reply. The receiver passes the same correlation_id to `wait_for_reply` to target the request. Omit for plain FYI sends.
