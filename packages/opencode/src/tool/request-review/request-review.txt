Declare your checkout ready for human review. Emits the review signal that
raises the lane in the review UI — nothing about your work is inferred; the
review moment exists because YOU declared it.

## When to use

- You are a checkout-homed session (started with an isolated workspace) and
  the deliverable is DONE: committed, verified, coherent as one unit.
- Integration work is finished: if you spawned subagents, you have already
  merged their branches into YOUR worktree and verified the integrated
  whole. One declaration covers the whole tree's work.

## When NOT to use

- You are a subagent. Your checkout rides your completion notification to
  your parent; the parent integrates your branch into THEIR worktree.
  Requesting review as a subagent is refused.
- Mid-work checkpoints. Commits during work are invisible to the human by
  design — the lane shows quiet telemetry until you declare. Do not declare
  to "show progress"; declare when tapping MERGE would be reasonable.
- Work you expect to keep changing. After you declare, the human may merge
  or discard at any moment — treat the checkout as hands-off until the
  verdict lands.

## The note

`note` becomes the review card's headline. One or two sentences: what this
lands, where to look first, anything that needs a decision. It is the only
context the reviewer gets besides the diff — write it like a good commit
subject, not a changelog.

## After declaring

Three verdicts are possible, none of them yours: merge (squash-lands your
branch, checkout dissolves), send back (the thread returns to you with the
reviewer's notes — address them and declare again), discard (dirty work is
snapshotted to a rescue commit first). Say what you need to say in the
note, then stop touching the checkout.
